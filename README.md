## Abstract

Gudraghat Lake in Badda, Dhaka represents the acute water-quality crisis affecting urban lakes across the Global South, exhibiting severe eutrophication, high biochemical oxygen demand (BOD), pathogenic contamination, solid-waste loading, and heavy-metal accumulation. This project repository hosts the documentation, technical specifications, and literature reviews for the **Integrated Biotechnological Remediation Model (IBRM)**.

The model combines physical pre-treatment, a five-zone phytoremediation belt, a horizontal subsurface-flow constructed wetland biofilter, microbial bioaugmentation with indigenous bacterial consortia, solar-powered aeration, and riparian restoration, anchored by trained community manpower and indigenous natural-resource utilization.

---

## Core System Architecture: The Five-Zone IBRM

The remediation framework is structured into five sequential functional zones designed for low-cost, local resource utilization and embedded community operation:

* **Zone 1 - Inlet Interception & Physical Pre-Treatment**: Modular bamboo-frame screens with synthetic mesh, community-operated floating booms, and manual skimming to remove solid and organic loads.


* **Zone 2 - Phytoremediation Belt**: A three-tier vegetated buffer around the lake perimeter utilizing emergent species (Typha angustata, Phragmites karka), floating species (Eichhornia crassipes, Pistia stratiotes), and submerged duckweed mats.


* **Zone 3 - Constructed Wetland Biofilter (CWB)**: A horizontal subsurface-flow constructed wetland (~1,200 to 1,500 square meters) using local gravel, WASA sludge biofilm inoculants, and Phragmites karka for organic degradation and pathogen removal.


* **Zone 4 - Open-Water Bioaugmentation**: Cultivation and reintroduction of indigenous bacterial consortia (Pseudomonas, Bacillus, Acinetobacter spp.) supported by solar-powered micro-diffusers for passive aeration.


* **Zone 5 - Riparian Restoration**: Shoreline stabilization and ecological buffering using native plant species (Hijol, Koroch, sedges, and rushes) to control erosion and limit direct human solid-waste dumping.

## Pilot Validation Protocol

To verify performance against Gudraghat's specific water chemistry prior to full-scale capital deployment, a controlled 90-day small-scale pilot protocol is specified:

* **System Design**: Ground-level 10 ft $\times$ 10 ft $\times$ 3 ft partitioned tank (~8,300 L) fed directly with lake water.


* **Monitoring Parameters**: Ten analytical metrics evaluated across baseline, Day 15, Day 30, Day 60, and Day 90, including BOD₅, dissolved oxygen, total nitrogen, total phosphorus, pH, total suspended solids, fecal coliform, heavy metals (ICP-OES), and odor panel scoring.


* **Decision Framework**: Explicit proceed, modify, or redesign triggers based on threshold target achievements across critical parameters.



---

## Resource and Cost Projections

* **Total Financial Outlay**: Projected at approximately BDT 1 crore (~USD 91,000) over a five-year horizon.


* **Manpower Requirements**: Full operational scale requires 35 to 45 personnel, with roughly 80 percent hired directly from the local Badda and Gudraghat communities.


* **Material Sourcing**: Approximately 90 percent of physical inputs (bamboo, river gravel, biochar, coir matting, and native seedlings) are sourced locally within Bangladesh.
