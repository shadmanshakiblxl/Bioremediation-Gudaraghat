Restoring Gudraghat Lake

An integrated biotechnological remediation model for urban lake renewal, prioritizing community manpower and indigenous natural resources in Dhaka, Bangladesh.

01 / The Problem Statement

Decades of unregulated urban discharge have transformed Gudraghat into an anoxic, eutrophic water body.

Biological & Organic Load

Heavy domestic sewage from adjacent settlements and industrial effluents drive severe biochemical oxygen demand depletion, toxic cyanobacterial blooms, and high pathogen concentrations across the aquatic ecosystem.

Chemical Accumulation

Urban runoff and direct dumping introduce persistent heavy metals into sediments, creating long-term toxicity reservoirs that conventional mechanical dredging fails to safely resolve.

02 / Literature Foundation

Grounded in limnological science and South Asian urban precedents.

The Integrated Biotechnological Remediation Model (IBRM) synthesizes decades of scholarly research on urban lake pollution biology, phytoremediation mechanisms, constructed wetland design, indigenous microbial bioaugmentation, and adaptive community governance.

Limnology & Biology

Wetzel (2001) and Paerl & Otten (2013) provide the foundational framework for nutrient cycling, biochemical oxygen dynamics, and harmful cyanobacterial bloom controls.

Phytoremediation

Rezania et al. (2019) and Vymazal (2013) document macrophyte uptake capacities using native species like Eichhornia crassipes, Typha angustata, and Phragmites karka.

Regional Precedents

Ghosh (2005) and Gupta & Singh (2015) demonstrate successful community-managed biological wastewater treatment across comparable South Asian megacities.

03 / The Solution Architecture

The Five-Zone Integrated Biotechnological Remediation Model

A sequential ecological intervention utilizing indigenous flora, constructed wetlands, microbial bioaugmentation, and solar aeration operated by a trained community cooperative.

Zone 01

Pre-Treatment & Inlet Interception

Bamboo coarse screens, synthetic mesh, and community-managed manual skimming intercept solid waste and floating plastics before reaching downstream biological stages.

Zone 02

Phytoremediation Belt

Three depth tiers utilizing Typha angustata, Phragmites karka, Eichhornia crassipes, and duckweed for nutrient, heavy-metal, and suspended solids uptake.

Zone 03

Constructed Wetland Biofilter

Horizontal subsurface-flow wetland with gravel media and indigenous WASA biofilm inoculants targeting rapid organic reduction and pathogen removal.

Zone 04

Open-Water Bioaugmentation

Locally isolated bacterial consortia (Pseudomonas, Bacillus, Acinetobacter) combined with solar-powered micro-diffusers to oxidize dissolved organic pollutants.

Zone 05

Riparian Restoration

Native tree planting including Hijol (Barringtonia acutangula) and Koroch (Pongamia pinnata) to stabilize shorelines, enhance biodiversity, and buffer human access.

04 / Model Verification

90-Day Controlled Pilot Protocol

Before full-scale lake deployment, a controlled small-scale tank test verifies performance against actual Gudraghat water chemistry across ten monitored parameters, ensuring strict empirical accountability.

05 / Resource Predictions

Five-Year Financial and Operational Forecast

The resource-prediction model forecasts full ecological restoration within BDT 1 crore (~USD 91,000) over five years, engaging 35-45 community personnel with ~80% local hiring.

Heavy-Metal Strategy

Because biological treatment alone cannot fully remove sediment-bound lead, mercury, and cadmium, a supplementary strategy combining enhanced phytoextraction and sediment immobilization is budgeted at BDT 20 lakh.

Community Governance

The Community Remediation Cooperative (CRC) operates local maintenance, composting, and monitoring, ensuring long-term institutional sustainability beyond initial project funding.
